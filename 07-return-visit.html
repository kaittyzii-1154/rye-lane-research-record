RETURN VISIT

SHOWING THE WEBSITE AND LISTENING TO THE RESPONSE

Taking the website
back to the shop.

I returned to Inverted Audio with the website and invited a response to the shop profile and the wider visual approach. The two recordings below capture parts of the same visit and include the same participant. They document a local conversation about the work, rather than two independent user tests.

01 / Looking through the shop profile

The participant browsed the photograph, directions, shop website and introduction video. He responded positively to the photograph and opened the directions link, which led to Google Maps. His clearest suggestion concerned the shop’s own website: the main link should lead to its homepage, so visitors could explore everything, rather than arriving only at the About page.

02 / Recognising the street through the drawing

In the second recording, the participant described the website as working well if the intention was minimalism. He also liked the artwork and could identify shops, houses and the roofline. This response encouraged me to retain the simple layout and recognisable architectural details in the street illustration.

What changed after the conversation

The main shop-website button was changed to the Inverted Audio homepage. The About page remains a separate background source. This is a small but specific change: the profile can introduce the shop while also helping a visitor continue into the shop’s own presentation of its work.

I retained the photographs, map link, introduction video and illustrated visual direction. The feedback supported keeping these elements for this participant; it did not establish that the website increased footfall or represented the wider community’s views.

What I still need to check

This visit focused on one shop page and an immediate response to the visual design. It did not test the whole navigation, walking routes, mobile experience or contribution process. A further visit could check the revised link and ask participants to find another shop or follow a walking prompt without my explanation. The response here begins a feedback cycle; the revised page has not yet been documented in a second review.
