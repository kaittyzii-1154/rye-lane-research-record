REFLECTION

LIMITATIONS AND WHAT COULD FOLLOW

What remains
unfinished.

A partial view of the street

The project connects observations and individual accounts, but its short interviews cannot represent everyone who uses Rye Lane. Photographs fix a moment, while drawings select and reorganise it. More sustained visits would help distinguish a temporary situation from a repeated pattern and bring in experiences currently missing.

Listening is not yet shared authorship

I continued to make the main choices about drawing, editing and the website. The record-shop owner’s suggestions offer a specific contribution, but do not establish shared control of the finished work. I also sometimes moved through questions too quickly. Returning to contributors, allowing longer answers and recording their corrections would make the relationship more reciprocal.

A next stage to test and develop

The website has not yet demonstrated how a wider audience understands or uses the research. Interview wording and the surrounding context also need a final listening check. The next stage could combine return visits with sharing the website through local shops and community contacts. Contributor-reviewed shop profiles would give people a more direct role in introducing their work and the area. A small viewer walkthrough could also explore whether the site helps people unfamiliar with Rye Lane understand it more closely. These steps would develop both the project’s reach and its relationship with the community.
